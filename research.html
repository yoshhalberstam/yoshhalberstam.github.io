Information and networks

Homophily, Group Size, and the Diffusion of Political Information in Social Networks: Evidence from Twitter

Journal of Public Economics, 2016 · with Brian Knight

In a network of 2.2 million politically engaged Twitter users and about 90 million connections, users in the larger political group received more information, sooner, and like-minded information reached like-minded users faster.

Network model; data on links, messages and exposure timing.

Measurement from voice

Gender Identity and Mode-Switching Behavior: Evidence from the Human Voice

Working paper, 2026

In about 40,000 three-second voicemail greetings from lawyers at top U.S. law firms, about a third of women switch between two pitch modes: their usual pitch and a second near where men's voices sit. Associates switch more than partners and counsel (43% vs. 31%), and listeners rate women who switch as less senior.

Pitch tracking, Gaussian mixture models, listener survey experiment.

Labor market institutions

Pay Transparency and the Gender Gap

AEJ: Applied Economics, 2023 · with Michael Baker, Kory Kroft, Alexandre Mas and Derek Messacar

Provincial laws made faculty salaries above a threshold public, adopted at different times across provinces. They narrowed the gender pay gap by 20 to 40%.

Administrative salary records; event-study design across institutions and departments.

The Impact of Unions on the Wage Distribution: Evidence from Higher Education

AER: Insights, 2026 · with Michael Baker, Kory Kroft, Alexandre Mas and Derek Messacar

Unionization raised salaries about 10% at the bottom of the distribution and left the top unchanged, consistent with contractual salary floors. Employment changed little; higher enrollment paid for the increase.

Staggered-rollout design; distributional analysis.
